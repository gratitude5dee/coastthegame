# ADR-0012 — Control passes: depth as one opacity-weighted image of splats and meshes, pose as an OpenPose figure on the root pose, camera.json per frame

**Status:** accepted (2026-09-07) · **Owner:** GRATITUD3 · **Refs:** goal.md STU-1, STU-2, GEN-6, ADR-0008, ADR-0010

## Context

A faithful render (Wan 2.2 VACE, the GEN table) follows the camera and the blocking only when it is handed control videos: depth and pose for the span it re-renders, and the camera per frame. STU-2 fixes the envelope — ≤ 5 s spans, ≤ 720p, 8-bit, short MP4/WebM control videos, never 16-bit PNG sequences — and names the sources: Spark's depth modifier for the splats, a depth texture for the meshes, an OpenPose-style skeleton, `camera.json` with intrinsics and extrinsics. At the initial checkpoint the actors were capsules with no bones to draw; the later rig-aware checkpoint below adds rendered joint readback. "Depth" for a splat scene is still not one well-defined surface.

## Decision

- **One depth image, opacity-weighted.** The depth pass (`packages/engine/src/render/depth.ts`) swaps every splat mesh's world modifier for one that writes the splat's distance along the view axis as its colour — same alpha, so the accumulated value is the opacity-weighted depth of what the picture shows, the "render depth" pattern — and swaps every opaque mesh's material for a shader that writes the same encoding; transparent meshes (fog sprites, translucent ghosts) and the sky are hidden and the clear colour is black. Encoding: **linear in metres between a per-clip near and far, near white** (`value = 1 − (z − near)/(far − near)`), stated in `camera.json` (`depth.encoding`, `near`, `far`) so a consumer can invert it or flip it to the inverse-depth convention its model prefers. Near/far default to 0.25 m / 60 m; the caller passes others. The pass renders straight to the canvas — no post stack, no grade — and restores everything it touched.
- **Pose is a figure on the root pose until there are bones.** `skeletonFor` (`packages/studio/src/control.ts`, pure) puts an 18-keypoint OpenPose (COCO) figure on an actor's feet, facing its yaw, scaled to its height, legs and arms swinging with its speed; the pass projects the keypoints with the frame's camera and draws OpenPose's limbs in OpenPose's colours on black. The ghosts performing on set and the people in the cell are the actors; a driving pose draws nothing (the car has no skeleton). When the bone tracks arrive (CHR-2, SCH-4) they replace `skeletonFor` and nothing else changes.
- **`camera.json` is per frame** — position, orientation (three's convention: the camera looks down −Z), vertical field of view and pinhole intrinsics in pixels (square pixels, principal point at the centre) — plus fps, size and the depth encoding. `projectPoint` in the same module reproduces three's projection exactly (unit-tested against a `PerspectiveCamera`), so a consumer can re-project without three.
- **Every exported frame waits for Spark.** Spark updates its splats asynchronously — a timeout after the render, an accumulate, a sort on a worker, and the picture switches to the new set only once _its_ sort is in (with LoD the set changes with the view, so it is always one sort behind live). A synchronous burst of `renderer.render` calls therefore draws the last live set: the same frame over and over, whatever the camera or the modifiers do. Both exporters now `settle` before drawing a frame (`Game.settleSplats`: `spark.update({ scene, camera })` awaited, then wait until the displayed accumulator is the current one; `autoUpdate` off for the duration). The depth pass needs it to show the depth modifier at all; the beauty export gains exact per-frame sorting for its moving camera.
- **Same set, same step, same camera as the beauty export.** `StudioSession.exportControl` drives the set with the beauty export's seek (the take's camera track or the keyframed path), plans the span with `planControl` (clipped to the cut, ≤ 5 s, the short side scaled to ≤ 720, even sizes) and encodes each pass with WebCodecs + Mediabunny like the cut (ADR-0008). `__coastExportControl` exposes it to QA; `preview` returns the last frame of each pass as a PNG.

## Reference package checkpoint (2026-09-07)

`StudioSession.exportControl` now defaults to matching **beauty + depth + pose** videos for the selected span and returns one local **ustar `.tar`** archive. The verdict card exposes Start/End seconds and a **Control package** button, followed by a separate **Download .tar** action. Explicit depth-only or pose-only calls remain supported. No files are uploaded and no generation job is submitted.

- **`hero.png` is the first beauty frame**, at the exact planned span start. It is captured after replay seek, camera setup and Spark settling, through the same host/post render as beauty. Beauty frame zero reuses those pixels, even if the caller orders beauty after depth. Selection/timing is deterministic; it is not a best-frame judge, and existing grain effects are not guaranteed byte-identical across separate renders.
- **Grounded prompts:** `packages/studio/src/controlPrompts.ts` builds `prompts.json` and `prompts.txt` from the rendered camera samples, selected takes, current cell/time/look and snapshotted avatar identities. Motion summaries sample only this span and clamp shorter takes at their endpoints. Mission titles are metadata, not evidence that requested lighting or motion occurred. Seedance/Veo/Kling/LTX/Wan drafts are plain-language variants, not verified endpoint payloads or a claim that all models accept depth/pose/camera inputs.
- **Portable archive:** fixed filenames for available videos, `hero.png`, `camera.json`, `prompts.json`, `prompts.txt`, and `package.json`. The package manifest records geometry/timing, the hero frame and SHA-256/byte sizes for every payload except itself. TAR uses fixed headers/zero modification times, requires no new dependency and preserves the original video bytes. Source URLs, preview data URLs and arbitrary extra metadata are excluded. Local capture media intentionally belongs in this explicitly requested download.
- **Bounds and cleanup:** callers may lower but not raise the five-second, 720-short-side, 1280-long-side limits; FPS is at most 60 and dimensions are even. Frames are floored to remain inside the requested span. Invalid/nonfinite inputs are rejected before renderer allocation. Encoder, PNG and depth failures restore renderer dimensions/DPR, camera pose/FOV/aspect, scene materials and playback. Export cannot start while recording, and retake/mission changes are blocked during export.
- **Rig-aware pose follow-up:** supported Mixamo instances now expose canonical world-space joints. The pose pass maps actual Arm/ForeArm/Hand, UpLeg/Leg/Foot and available Neck/Eye joints to OpenPose slots. It does not invent nose or ear locations; absent landmarks remain empty. Known unsupported/unrigged assets produce no skeleton; only legacy actors without rig information use `skeletonFor`. `poseSources` counts rig, procedural and omitted actor-frame samples, including offscreen candidates, in the export result and package manifest. This is readback of the rendered rig, not optical motion capture or occlusion-aware pose estimation.
- **Remaining limits:** skeletal replay phase is still reconstructed rather than recorded per bone. Beauty is silent and uncaptioned in this control package. Private upload/retention, production provider payload adapters, automatic submission, faithful generation and visual-quality acceptance remain separate work.

## Consequences

- - A faithful render can be requested today with the beauty clip, the two control videos and the camera file for any ≤ 5 s span of a cut — the fal job (GEN-6) only has to upload them.
- - Depth follows what the splats actually show (soft edges, foliage) rather than a proxy collider; the meshes (props, the car, the ghosts) land in the same image at the same scale.
- − Opacity-weighted depth is not a surface: where splats are translucent the value blends front and back. The collider GLBs (M2) offer a crisp alternative for cells that have one — a `depthSource: 'collider'` option if a model wants it.
- − Rig-joint readback reflects the currently sampled skeleton, but full bone-motion recording and arbitrary authored performance playback remain pending. Legacy procedural figures still constrain only position, scale and gait.
- − `normal` / `id` passes (optional in STU-2) are not rendered yet; `PassName` reserves them.
