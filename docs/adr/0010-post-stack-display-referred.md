# ADR-0010 — The post stack works on the finished picture: display-referred buffers, looks as baked LUTs, export through the stack on every tier

**Status:** accepted (2026-09-07) · **Owner:** GRATITUD3 · **Refs:** goal.md W-5, MIS-6, STU-4, AF-7, PLT-1 (the tier table's post-processing row), QB-3

## Context

W-5 makes time of day a grade in four layers — the per-splat dyno modifier, a post LUT, the sky dome, the lights — and MIS-6 / STU-4 want the mission to name a look, "the colour LUT named by the mission's look", on the live picture and on the cut. AF-7 fixes the library: `postprocessing`. The question was where the stack should work. `postprocessing` (like every three.js post pipeline) assumes a _linear_ scene buffer: the scene is rendered into a half-float or 8-bit render target, effects run in linear light, and the last pass encodes to sRGB. Spark does not fit that: a splat scene was trained by alpha-blending its stored (sRGB-ish) colours straight into the frame, and Spark reproduces that on the canvas by writing those values and blending in that space. Rendered into a linear target (Spark's `encodeLinear`), the splats blend in linear light and soft edges, fog-like splats and every translucent overlap come out lighter than the scan — a different picture from the one on every other tier, which renders to the canvas.

## Decision

- **Display-referred buffers.** The composer's buffers are RGBA8 targets that three treats the way it treats the canvas: the target is flagged as one three would take for an XR layer (`isXRRenderTarget`) with an sRGB colour space, so three's materials encode to sRGB in-shader, and its storage is pinned to `RGBA8` (three r180 would otherwise allocate `SRGB8_ALPHA8` for a texture attachment and the hardware would encode a second time — measured as ±1 noise and skipped levels before the pin). Spark keeps `encodeLinear = false`: it writes and blends sRGB values exactly as on screen; the sky, the props, the ghosts land next to them looking exactly as they do without the stack. The effect passes never encode or decode (`encodeOutput` off; the LUT is told its input is already display values; the buffer is read through a `mediump` sampler, not the `lowp` one `postprocessing` picks for byte buffers, whose 1/256 steps would put ±1 noise on every pixel where the GPU honours it), and the last pass hands the pixels to the canvas as they are. **A `clean` look at noon is the direct render, to the bit** — `tests/e2e/post.spec.ts` captures the same frame through the stack and straight to the canvas and requires identical bytes, and the screenshot baselines made before the stack existed still pass through it.
- **Looks are recipes baked to a 32³ LUT on the CPU** (`packages/engine/src/render/looks.ts`, import-free, unit-tested): ASC-CDL lift · gamma · gain, contrast about middle grey, saturation, split toning, a faded print — plus the amounts the stack reads directly (vignette, grain, chromatic aberration, how much bloom the look lets through). Five ship: `clean`, `35mm-dusk`, `vhs-1994`, `noir`, `neon-night`; the director's schema, the grammar and the missions name the same list. The time-of-day preset contributes a contrast + lift _under_ the look (`Grade.post`, tweened with the rest of the grade), and the LUT is rebaked (~1 ms) whenever either moves. Bloom follows the preset's `neon` × the look's bloom, and its pass drops out entirely when there is nothing to bloom. No LUT files: a look is data in the repo, the same numbers the provenance manifest names.
- **Live only where the budget says so; export everywhere.** Desktop (`postProcessing: 'full'`) renders through the stack live; `?post=0/1` overrides for QA. Quest, phones, Vision Pro keep the per-splat grade and render to the canvas (`'grade'`), and XR never goes through the composer (it draws to the layer). The cut export is offline (STU-1) and not frame-budgeted, so **every tier renders the cut through the stack**: the exporter takes a `render` hook, the host borrows a stack for the export and drops it after. The 2D compositor keeps only the captions; the CSS-filter "look" it applied before is gone. The manifest records the look that was actually rendered (the director may have changed it from the mission's: _make it noir_).
- **On demand, its own chunk.** `postprocessing` is not on the boot path (QB-3): `@coast/engine/post` is a separate entry the app imports dynamically when the stack is first needed (desktop boot, or the first export elsewhere).

## Consequences

- - Splats look identical with and without the stack; the looks are what the art direction dials, not a side effect of the pipeline.
- - Looks are pure data with a unit-tested transform; the manifest, the mission card and the director all name the same five.
- - Phones and headsets pay nothing live and still export the graded cut.
- − Effects that want linear light (physically-motivated bloom, exposure, tone mapping) work on display values here; the bloom is an LDR bloom with a luminance threshold, and Spark's own DoF (`focalDistance` / `apertureAngle`, CAM-6) stays the depth-of-field path.
- − The buffer trick leans on a three internal (`isXRRenderTarget`, r180 pinned): a three upgrade re-checks `WebGLPrograms` / `WebGLTextures` for the flag — `screenshot.spec.ts` fails loudly if the encoding path changes.
- − Grain is animated by the pass clock; a still is only deterministic with a grain-free look (the screenshot harness uses `clean`).
